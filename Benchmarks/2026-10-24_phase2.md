# Phase 2 benchmark: real models vs Flatpack cards

Date: 2026-10-24. Engine 26.10.13b. RTX 3090, Ryzen 9 9950X. Editor play mode, `scenes/flatpack_benchmark.scene`
(one shadowed sun, sky, envmap, ground plane). Raw numbers: `Benchmarks/raw/2026-10-24_phase2_bench.jsonl`.

## Method

For each model we spawn a square grid of instances (spacing 2.2 bounding radii, random yaw), point the main camera
at the whole grid, and run it twice: every Flatpack Renderer forced to the real model, then forced to the card.
After 3 seconds to settle we read GPU time from `perf_overview` (average over the last 2 seconds) and triangles and
draw calls from `perf_rendering`. GPU time is the honest number here: the editor caps the frame at 90 fps when
unfocused, so frame time sits at 11.1 ms in most runs. The empty scene costs **1.21 ms** GPU on its own.

Atlases are 2048 x 2048, 8 x 8 hemisphere frames. Models are the high-poly props used by Orion SCP-RP, installed
from sbox.game.

## Reading it

- Cards cost about the same whatever the source: 0.1 to 0.8 ms per few hundred on screen, set by pixels covered,
  not triangles. The saving grows with the source's triangle count; the 975k-triangle bed goes from 21 fps to the cap.
- Cards from one impostor instance: 100 or 400 cards add only a handful of draw calls.
- The far tree loses. `tree_small_01.vmdl` ships its own LOD chain that ends in a 2-triangle billboard, so at
  7,000 units the engine already draws almost nothing, and an impostor pixel shader costs more than a plain
  billboard. Flatpack pays off on models without a cheap last LOD, or as a better-looking replacement for one.
- Not measured yet: cross-fade cost, the distance pass with thousands of renderers, 4096 atlases, VRAM.
